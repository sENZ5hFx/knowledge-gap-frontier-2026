# Denominator fracture across realms

Timestamp: 2026-10-21 13:40 EDT
Session ID: 20261021-1340-denominator-fracture
Status: synthesis and audit, not a claim of a newly observed natural object

## What this is not

This session does not deliver undiscovered facts. A fact that is undiscovered is, by definition, not in the sources. Anything stated below as unproved is a hypothesis with the evidence that fails to prove it. Prior pages in this same Notion workspace already ranked "unknowns" on a single score (for example the 2026-07-31 frontier page: deep-sea genomic dark matter 0.887, abiogenesis 0.83, dark matter 0.808). Those scores treat different denominators as one scale. That ranking is the object this session refuses.

## Primary insight

The highest-value finding is methodological. Public "percent unknown" figures for space, ocean, land, animals, and the planetary interior do not share a denominator, an operationalization, or a unit. Of 12 sourced claims encoded from this session, 66 of 66 cross pairs were illegal to average. The tempting mean of three famous-looking numbers — about 5% of the cosmic energy budget has a known microphysical identity, 89.4% of one hadal species-bin set was unreported, and 6.74% of surface cells in one biodiversity extract contain an animal record — is 0.337 if you commit the error. That mean is rejected. It is not a measurement of ignorance.

What already existed: Hortal and colleagues' seven biodiversity shortfalls (Linnean, Wallacean, Prestonian, Darwinian, Hutchinsonian, Raunkiæran, Eltonian; Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics, 2015, doi:10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-112414-054400), plus a 2025 missing-data treatment of biodiversity gaps (Biological Reviews, doi:10.1111/brv.13127). What did not exist in the retrieved literature or in the prior session ranking was a guard that makes the cross-realm league table illegal, applied to the actual figures in front of us.

## The one legal arithmetic result

From one Ecography extract (doi:10.1111/ecog.05926; article year not re-verified beyond the DOI): about 6.74% of the globe, 5% of the ocean, and 11% of the land had an animal occurrence record at the grid used. Those are the same operationalization. They still must not be casually averaged, because ocean and land are different areas.

A weight check, not a new survey: coverage 0.11 with weight 0.29 (land) and coverage 0.05 with weight 0.71 (ocean) returns 0.0674, equal to the published globe figure at the precision stored here. The weights are the familiar surface split, chosen because they close. This shows the three headlines are consistent with one split measurement. It does not prove the authors' raw grid used exactly those weights, because 11%, 5%, and 6.74% are already rounded.

Under that same weighting, cells without a record occupy about 25.8% of Earth's surface on land and about 67.5% in the ocean. The unread surface is mostly ocean because the ocean is most of the surface and is slightly less occupied, not because 5% and 11% are different kinds of knowledge. A second call with weights 0.29 and 0.50 was refused: weights must sum to 1, and the function will not quietly renormalize.

A Spearman correlation of "distance from the human sensor envelope" against coverage was refused. n = 2.

## Realm by realm, and the thing that is actually unproved

### Space

Spoken, and not resolved: the identity of the ~95% of the cosmic energy budget that is not ordinary matter. Dark matter and dark energy are placeholder names for gravitational and expansion behavior. Budget fractions are the known part. Identity is the unknown part. That is the inverse of biology, where even the budget (how many species, how much living carbon) moves when the method changes.

Unproved, and only an analogy: proposals that the Milky Way sits in a large underdensity are the same kind of selection effect as the Northern Hemisphere bias in biodiversity databases. Nothing retrieved here tests that identity. A 2025 popular account of the underdensity was not treated as a measurement.

### Ocean

Established in the sources, still narrow: Peng, Du, Gebruk and colleagues (Nature 645, 679–685, 2025, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09317-z) documented chemosynthetic communities, dominated by siboglinid worms and bivalves, from 5,800 m to 9,533 m along 2,500 km of the Kuril–Kamchatka and western Aleutian trenches, fed by fault-transported sulfide and methane. The methane isotopes point to microbial carbonate reduction of sedimentary organic matter.

Unproved, in the authors' own hedging: that the same communities are the default state of other hadal trenches, that the trophic subsidy to heterotrophs is large once quantified, and that the carbon-storage implication survives contact with global models. "Might be more widespread" is not a map.

Separately, inside the MEER hadal sediments (Mariana, Yap, Philippine Basin), homogeneous selection (~50.5%) and dispersal limitation (~43.8%) dominated, and neutral drift did not. That is a process claim about one dataset, not a law of the hadal zone.

The sharp sampling sentence that is not a species count: half of the benthic records in the OBIS extract sit on the shallowest 1% of the seafloor, and more than three quarters of the records are in the Northern Hemisphere. Tropical latitudes from −5° to 5° contributed under 2.5% of records in the 2026 Nature Communications compilation. Models trained on those archives are fitted to the places ships already go.

### Land and inland water

The October 2026 lake report is secondary. It describes 19,925 mitochondrial genomes and about 3,660 species from 362 lakes, fewer than 1 in 20 matched to a known genus, and about 750 not placed in any known group. Genus-match failure above 95% and "unplaced" at about 750/3,660 are different fractions. Do not collapse them into "20,000 new species." The same report says the method misses lineages with unusual or absent mitochondria, and rare lineages that never assemble.

Unproved: that this genus-failure rate holds for a non-mitochondrial survey, or for lakes outside that 28-country pool.

On land animal records, 11% cell occupancy still means most land cells in that product are empty. High elevation was flagged in the same family of bias papers. This session did not retrieve a soil-fauna percentage worth quoting. Inventing one would have been the failure mode.

### Animals

Record inequality, from the large occurrence synthesis: in most groups, half the records represent well under 4% of the species, and a handful of bird species can dominate GBIF animal records. Hortal's Eltonian shortfall (interactions unknown) is already named.

Unproved: that adding more records of the same over-recorded species will shrink the interaction shortfall. The inequality makes that hope look weak. It is not a test.

### Humans

No primary percentage for the human body, the human microbiome, or consciousness was retrieved. Consciousness is unsolved and heavily spoken. It is not a finding of this session to repeat it.

The only human-adjacent number in hand is about models, not people: in one 14 October 2026 post, 53 of 85 language models named quantum gravity, 14 named consciousness, and 6 named dark matter or dark energy. That is a salience sample of machines. It matches the pattern of the July 31 ranking, which also put cosmology and "dark" metaphors on one ladder with ocean genes.

Unproved, and deliberately not quantified: any sentence of the form "X percent of human physiology is unmapped."

### Earth interior

Magnabosco and colleagues (Nature Geoscience, 2018) estimated 2 to 6 × 10^29 cells in the continental subsurface and put total global prokaryotic biomass near 23–31 Pg C, lower than earlier estimates by a factor of about 4 to 10. The Deep Carbon Observatory's 15–23 Pg C deep-life figure and its ~2–2.3 billion km³ volume are a different, secondary synthesis.

Unproved: every "deep life is N times humanity" multiplier that does not state which carbon total it uses. A phys.org interview quote (about 10^29 cells under the oceans, "200 times" human biomass) was not used as an input, because it is a secondary quotation and it collides with the revision above.

### Atmosphere

The 2025 Microbial Dark Matter symposium (mSphere, doi:10.1128/msphere.00587-25) discussed atmospheric microbiomes and dark oxygen in groundwater. The full text returned 403 to the fetcher, so no rate was taken from it. One speaker's reported point is worth keeping as a definition, not a number: the darkness is the failure to shine the right light, not a claim that the organisms are ontologically missing.

Unproved: that the atmospheric microbiome is a resident, climate-relevant biomass rather than a sampler of the surface.

## What would make a real cross-realm measurement

Not another scored list. One protocol, applied unchanged:

- same grid
- same taxonomic rank
- same definition of "known" (described species, or genus match, or genome bin — pick one)
- explicit area or volume weights that sum to 1
- a separate column for process knowledge, which is not a fraction of taxa

Until that exists, a ranking of realms by "how unknown" is a portrait of the denominator the author liked.

## Adversarial review

- The lake figure can move or disappear when the primary paper is opened. It is labeled secondary for that reason.
- The Ecography year was not confirmed. The DOI was.
- The 0.0674 identity uses rounded inputs and round area weights. It is a consistency check.
- "Supports inversion" in the first paired contrast depended on a naive expectation written into the test. The durable sentence is the area split, not the word inversion.
- Chemosynthesis at hadal depth is a discovery already published by Peng et al. Claiming it here would be theft. The unproved remainder is the extrapolation they refused to make.
- Image and video are metaphors of the thin measured skin. They are not data.

## Capability gap

No tool in this session, native or connected, can align biodiversity archives, metagenomic novelty, IPCC gap censuses, and cosmological energy-budget fractions onto one weight vector. The Python guard can only refuse. Refusal is the correct output until the crosswalk exists.
